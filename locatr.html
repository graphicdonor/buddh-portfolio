01 / Problem

Grocery shopping is surprisingly stressful

The average grocery shopping trip involves 27 items and takes 41 minutes — but studies show 25% of that time is spent wandering aisles looking for specific products. Stores change their layouts, products move, and shoppers without a systematic approach backtrack constantly.

🗺️
No map of the store
Shoppers rely on memory and trial-and-error to navigate store layouts they may only visit occasionally — or that have changed since their last visit.
🔄
Products frequently move
Promotions, restocking rotations, and seasonal displays mean the product you found last week may not be in the same place today.
📋
Lists don't connect to location
You know what you need, not where it is. Shopping lists are purely item-based — there's no connection between your list and the physical store layout.
🙋
Store staff are hard to find for help
Locating an available member of staff who knows product locations adds time and frustration — especially during busy periods.
02 / Research

Understanding the shopper journey

I conducted contextual inquiry sessions with 8 shoppers across 3 different store formats — hypermarket, supermarket, and convenience. I observed the full journey from arrival to checkout, noting navigation patterns, list-checking behaviours, and moments of friction.

Mental Models
Shoppers organise by meal, not by category. They think "ingredients for pasta night" not "pasta aisle then sauce aisle then cheese section" — the mental model doesn't match the store structure.
Navigation Heuristics
Most people do a rough perimeter sweep first, then fill in the middle aisles. This intuitive pattern suggests a route optimisation that respects how shoppers naturally move.
List Behaviour
Most people check their list 8–12 times per trip. This constant context-switching between list and environment is a core friction point — the list and the map should be one.
03 / Design Solution

Three layers of wayfinding

LocatR collapses the gap between your shopping list and the physical store through three interconnected layers — each designed to reduce cognitive load at a specific moment in the trip.

Smart List Builder
Add items normally. LocatR automatically groups them by aisle, optimises the path through the store, and shows which items are available vs out of stock.
Store Map View
Bird's eye view of the store with your items pinned. Colour-coded by section (produce, dairy, frozen etc.). Current position tracked via Bluetooth beacon or Wi-Fi triangulation.
Aisle Navigator
Tap any item to get step-by-step aisle directions from your current position, with a shelf-level indicator (eye level, bottom shelf, above eye line).
Live Availability
Inventory sync shows items as available (green), low stock (amber), or unavailable (red). Auto-suggests alternatives for out-of-stock items before you reach the aisle.
04 / Key Design Decisions

Designing for a distracted context

Grocery shoppers are cognitively loaded — pushing a cart, managing kids, checking prices, and trying to remember what else they need. Every design decision was made for a distracted, moving user.

01
Glanceable UI
Large text, high contrast, and one-action screens. You should be able to understand the screen in under 1 second without stopping your cart. No hidden menus, no dense information panels.
02
Voice-First Option
Hands-free mode lets shoppers add items and navigate by voice — critical when your hands are full or you're pushing a cart. Voice commands are simple and forgiving of ambient noise.
03
Offline-Capable
Store map and your list work offline after the initial load. Connectivity in large stores can be poor — the app can't fail mid-trip. All core functionality is available without a network.
04
Minimal Auth
No account required to use the basic map and list. Account unlocks history, preferences, and personalised suggestions. Reduce friction at entry — the first trip should require zero sign-up.
05 / Outcome

Turning grocery shopping into a system

LocatR transforms an unstructured, memory-dependent activity into a guided, efficient system — giving shoppers the spatial intelligence of a store associate, always in their pocket.
