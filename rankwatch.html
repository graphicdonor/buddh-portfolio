01 / Problem

Rich data. Poor signal.

RankWatch had market-leading SEO data — real-time rank tracking across 100 SERPs, a patent-pending backlink crawler powered by Majestic, and a site auditor covering 110+ parameters. But the product surface made it nearly impossible for users to act on that data. The interface buried insight under volume, forcing expert interpretation just to understand what was happening with a client's site.

Rank Tracker Data Overload
Current rank, highest rank, city rank, initial rank, and search volume — all dumped into a flat table with no hierarchy. Users couldn't see at a glance what actually changed or what to act on.
Backlink Data Without Narrative
Raw backlink counts, referring domains, and anchor text ratios from Majestic with no trend context. Link health status was invisible — users had to manually cross-reference tables to understand acquisition or loss.
Audit Results with No Priority
110+ audit parameters dumped in a single list — broken links, missing meta tags, slow load times, and minor warnings treated identically. No way to separate critical issues from informational noise.
Fragmented Agency Reporting
White-label reports required 20+ steps to configure. No shared dashboards, no scheduled exports, no client-facing views. Every report was a manual production effort, killing agency productivity.
02 / Approach

Surface insight, not raw data

I started with a module-by-module audit — interviewing agency account managers, mapping every task they performed per session, and identifying where data volume caused paralysis instead of action. The brief was clear: RankWatch's data advantage only mattered if users could act on it without becoming analysts themselves.

Signal Before Volume
Every module redesign started with one question: what does the user need to decide here? Trend, threshold, and change indicators came before raw numbers — not after.
Severity-Led Information Architecture
Site audit and backlink data were restructured around impact, not category. Critical issues float to the top; informational findings are accessible but not competing for attention.
Agency-First Reporting
White-label setup, scheduled exports, and client-shareable views were treated as first-class features — not afterthoughts. Reporting had to be something agencies could delegate, not perform.
03 / Key Decisions

Four modules. Four design shifts.

01
Rank Tracker — From Table to Timeline
Replaced flat position tables with timeline-first rank views. Current rank, highest rank, city rank, and initial rank were reframed as a single position story with movement indicators and threshold alerts — not five separate columns. Users could now see at a glance whether a keyword was climbing, stable, or at risk.
Top 100 SERP coverageCity & local rank visibilityMovement threshold alerts
02
Backlink Monitor — Health Before Volume
Surfaced link health status — active, lost, new — as the primary layer before raw counts. Majestic-powered crawler data was visualised as acquisition trend charts, anchor text distribution rings, and TLD breakdowns, giving agencies a narrative they could act on, not a spreadsheet they had to interpret.
Active / lost / new link statesAcquisition trend chartsAnchor text distribution
03
Site Audit — Priority Over Category
Restructured 110+ audit parameters into a severity-first hierarchy: Critical, Warnings, and Informational — each with an estimated impact score. Broken links and missing meta tags no longer sat beside minor schema warnings. Agencies could triage a full audit in minutes, not hours of scrolling.
110+ parametersCritical / Warning / Info tiersImpact scoring
04
White-Label Reporting — Delegatable by Design
Reduced white-label report setup from a 20-step configuration process to a 3-step workflow: brand it, pick modules, schedule it. Built shareable client dashboards, scheduled PDF email digests, and a Low Hanging Fruits report template that agencies could send to prospects as an instant value demo.

04 / Design System

Built for SEO data at density

The component library was purpose-built for the demands of an SEO platform — not adapted from a generic UI kit. Every component was designed to handle dense data tables, multi-series charts, and comparison states across M, L, and XL plan tiers without breaking down at scale.

SEO Data Visualisation
Position sparklines, rank movement badges (↑ ↓ –), trend overlays, and comparison charts purpose-built for SERP data. Designed to communicate direction at a glance — not just absolute values.
Audit Finding Cards
A card system with severity indicators (Critical / Warning / Info), impact scores, and expandable fix guidance. Designed so any account manager — not just an SEO specialist — could triage a full audit.
Rank & Backlink Tables
Sortable, filterable, paginated tables with inline status badges, bulk export, and column configuration — the workhorse of the platform, used across Rank Tracker, Backlinks, and Keyword Research.
White-Label Report Templates
Modular report layouts covering keyword performance, site health, backlink health, competitor analysis, and Low Hanging Fruits. Agency-brandable, scheduled, and exportable as PDF with a single click.
05 / Outcome

From data warehouse to insight engine

The overhaul transformed RankWatch from a powerful but opaque data platform into a product where every user — from agency owner to junior account manager — could extract value without becoming an SEO analyst first. The data advantage was always there; the design job was to make it visible.
